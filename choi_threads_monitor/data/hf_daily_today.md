### 1. K2-Horizon: A Frontier Fleet of Open Source Language Models
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21003
Upvotes: 51

We present K2-Horizon, a fleet of six fully open language models, from a 0.9B dense model to a 375B-A23B MoE, built for general-purpose reasoning and agentic work across math and science, software engineering, deep research, office automation, and beyond. The models lead or are competitive with strong open models of their size on agentic and coding benchmarks. On the modeling side, to strengthen reasoning, we introduce Mixture-of-Value Attention (MoVA), applying MoE to attention value projections, and softplus attention gating, a more flexible, theoretically motivated gate. Our 36B-A4B model uses both and nearly matches a 32B dense model trained on the same data, with one-seventh the active parameters. We also introduce Uno, lossless diffusion adapters that speed up decoding by up to 2.2x without a separate draft model. Our release follows the LLM360 definition of open source for transparency and reproducibility: the full development history, including intermediate checkpoints from every training stage, pre-training data, training logs, and all training, evaluation, and infrastructure code, notably our production-scale xLLM framework for dense and MoE training and the RL360 reinforcement-learning stack. Beyond the data itself, we release and document in detail every pipeline that built it, the part of model development that open releases most rarely disclose. These pipelines produced roughly 10T synthetic pre-training tokens, showing that synthetic data can be a major ingredient of pre-training at scale. Four of the models share one 22T-token pre-training corpus and recipe, revealing consistent scaling behavior and enabling controlled studies of scale and sparsity. All artifacts are available at https://ifm.ai/k2/

### 2. AllocFlow: End-to-End Portfolio Trading Action Generation from Raw Market Data
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.19937
Upvotes: 51

Real-world portfolio management requires converting noisy market observations into coordinated, simultaneous allocations across multiple assets under shared capital constraints. Advances in forecasting models and alpha discovery help identify opportunities. However, traditional pipelines often decouple signal forecasting from portfolio construction, potentially misaligning learning with allocation and execution objectives. We present AllocFlow, an end-to-end flow-matching framework that directly generates joint portfolio allocations from raw multi-asset market histories. To capture dependencies among portfolio positions, AllocFlow employs a cross-asset flow Transformer conditioned on temporal market representations to generate continuous, signed position weights across all instruments jointly. To address the sample inefficiency and instability of learning continuous allocation policies from scratch, we establish a two-stage training scheme: strategy-supervised imitation shapes a structured distribution of candidate portfolios, followed by group-relative reinforcement learning (RL) that refines joint decisions using portfolio-level financial rewards and a frozen policy reference. On hourly cryptocurrency perpetual futures, RL refinement raises the annualized net return of the imitation policy from 27.79% to 51.38% and its Calmar ratio from 0.955 to 4.043, surpassing the teacher strategy on every metric, and AllocFlow reaches a Calmar ratio of 4.90 on six further months of unseen 2026 data without retraining.

### 3. Sage S2.1 Flash Technical Report
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21072
Upvotes: 41

We introduce Sage S1.5 Flash, a 320-billion-parameter general-purpose mixture-of-experts model designed to reason over scientific evidence and act in interactive environments. Sage Sci-Perception uses dedicated encoders for molecular graphs and biological sequences and represents spectra as ordered patches of measurements. Modality-specific projectors map these representations into the input space of a shared backbone, which processes them alongside text and images. To develop the model's agent capabilities, we construct verifiable environments for tool use, search, and coding, as well as specialized workflows involving machine learning experiments and scientific software. Task completion is checked against execution results, changes in environment state, or submitted artifacts. We evaluate Sage S1.5 Flash on 33 benchmarks across seven domains. In the primary comparison against five baselines, it ranks first on OfficeQA Pro (72.2), MLE-bench Lite (90.3 HumanRank), Harvey LAB, and SpreadsheetBench~2, and second on OSWorld-Verified (85.2), WebArena-Verified (67.6), and DeepResearch Bench~II (54.2). These results show particular strengths in using documentary evidence, producing verifiable workbooks, and carrying out machine learning tasks, alongside competitive performance in desktop and browser workflows that require the model to act on visual feedback.

### 4. InclusionMed: Benchmarking AI for Inclusive Healthcare
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21218
Upvotes: 39

Advancing medical AI for inclusive healthcare requires evaluation across diverse healthcare roles, tasks, and care settings, but benchmark coverage remains uneven. We introduce InclusionMed, an open medical AI evaluation initiative for inclusive healthcare. The InclusionMed Atlas organizes benchmarks across three healthcare roles and three AI capabilities. For the first release, we selected four public benchmarks from 388 candidates through structured audits and independent reproduction. With InclusionMed-DxFiles, our private diagnostic reasoning benchmark, the Atlas contains five benchmarks covering three of its nine role--capability cells. We evaluated frontier large language models on the Atlas and aggregated their results into the InclusionMed Index, with overall scores ranging from 17.5 to 49.1 out of 100. The Index also reports scores by healthcare role and AI capability, showing that a model's rank varied with the role or capability considered. A within-benchmark analysis using task-level tags showed that the ten highest-ranked models in the Index all scored higher on patient communication than on clinical decision-making. Models with similar Index scores also differed substantially in estimated inference cost. These findings highlight the need for evaluation covering more diverse healthcare needs and for context-specific model selection. Through open collaboration and community contributions, InclusionMed seeks to ensure that diverse healthcare needs worldwide shape how medical AI progress is defined and measured.

### 5. SWE-CoWork: Benchmarking Coding Agents as Digital Employees in Organizations
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20218
Upvotes: 27

A coding agent working within a software organization must establish what to build, collaborate with colleagues, and verify what it delivers. We introduce SWE-CoWork, a benchmark comprising 101 development assignments across 101 real repositories, 11 programming languages, and 12 application domains. Each assignment places an agent in a simulated engineering organization with 26 colleagues who have distinct responsibilities, bounded knowledge, and controlled collaboration behavior. Starting from a brief handoff, agents must recover requirements distributed across colleagues and workplace records, reconcile conflicting evidence, and implement changes evaluated by private executable tests. We evaluate eleven models and find a substantial gap between partial implementation and complete task delivery. The two leading models satisfy approximately 63% of fail-to-pass (F2P) tests but complete fewer than 8% of assignments. Providing complete requirements upfront improves F2P by approximately 16 percentage points for Seed Evolving and GPT-6 Astra, yet leaves complete task success unchanged. Requirement-level trajectory analysis reveals failures throughout the delivery process: Claude-Opus-5.5 directs a mean of 93% of first questions about queried cards to their accountable owners across runs but obtains only 64% of colleague-held requirements. Deferred questions go unfollowed, implementations remain incomplete even after requirements are acquired, and 31% of requirements reported as completed fail their tests. These findings highlight sustained requirement tracking, from clarification through implementation, verification, and handoff, as a key challenge for reliable autonomous software engineering.

### 6. SPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents against Prompt Injection
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07222
Upvotes: 27

Tool-using agents act on email, files, and payments, so an instruction hidden in a web page or a shared document can make an agent send data or money to an attacker. Many runtime defenses put a language model on the decision that lets an action through, so the decision depends on a model that may read the attacker's text and adds model calls to every step. Provenance defenses keep the model off the final check but need a model to plan or route each task first. Where an action goes can instead be read from the tools' typed returns: an email's sender or a payment's account arrives in a typed field, while injected instructions arrive as free text. SPILLWAY turns this observation into a deterministic floor with no per-task plan. The check admits a call when each target value that appears in tool output but not in the user's task was returned by a read-only tool in a typed field or is covered by a fixed delegation rule. A deterministic sanitizer removes instruction-like regions its fixed rules match before the model reads them and retains the removed spans as evidence, and a local judge may restore a refused call, never one whose target lies in the removed text. Across three backbones, and seven task suites on one of them, no standard templated injection redirects an action to an attacker's target under SPILLWAY, at the highest observed mean utility and composite scores (NRP, ERS) of the defenses we run under that attack. A forged chat template also produces a raw zero, and mean in-scope attack success under a released reinforcement-learning attacker is .007. SPILLWAY reaches this floor with no defense-triggered backbone re-run and almost no added model call. As personal AI agents act on users' email, files, and payments, SPILLWAY gives them an auditable refusal floor on where their actions go that keeps them useful and holds across backbones.

### 7. ScaleVLA: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models to 552 Billion Parameters
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21607
Upvotes: 19

Frontier Vision-Language Models (VLMs) such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in robot control, suggesting that broad pretraining provides knowledge valuable for physical action. Yet most Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies still rely on much smaller VLM backbones, leaving this potential largely untapped. We ask whether frontier-scale representations can provide a stronger foundation for robot learning. We introduce ScaleVLA, which connects a 552B DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash backbone to a trainable action DiT, and systematically compare VLMs from 2B to 552B under a shared training setup. We evaluate three fundamental capabilities of robot foundation models. In sample efficiency, just 10 demonstrations outperform a 2B backbone trained with 50 (87.8% vs. 85.6%). In generalization, LIBERO-PRO success reaches 45.1%, compared with at most 31.3% for smaller backbones. In precise manipulation, real-world charger insertion improves from 4/10 to 9/10 successes, with gains concentrated in contact-sensitive alignment. Despite its scale, ScaleVLA achieves 216 ms median latency per action chunk. Our findings suggest that frontier VLMs can serve not only as reasoning agents, but also as powerful representational foundations for efficient and generalizable robot policies.

### 8. VoxMel TTS Technical Report
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20254
Upvotes: 19

Continuous autoregressive TTS relies on an acoustic target that enables stable next-latent modeling while preserving fine-grained acoustic details. While existing approaches employ learned compressed representations (e.g., continuous VAE latents) to render prediction tractable, they rely on complex neural encoders that often cap generation fidelity. Conversely, training-free targets like mel-spectrograms eliminate encoder overhead, yet their potential for autoregressive generation remains largely underexplored. In this work, we propose VoxMel, a tokenizer-free framework for autoregressive TTS that makes stable mel modeling tractable through a fixed, exactly invertible two-axis Haar transform. The transform first decomposes each mel spectrogram along frequency into local averages and a frequency residual, then splits the averaged component along time into Coarse-Mel and a temporal residual. A language-model backbone with a flow-matching head autoregressively generates Coarse-Mel. Non-autoregressive residual tiers then predict the temporal residual from Coarse-Mel, followed by the frequency residual conditioned on the reconstructed Frequency-Averaged Mel. Neither residual is fed back into the autoregressive history. Trained on 100K hours of Emilia, the supervised 0.6B model obtains 1.51\% WER and 0.738 speaker similarity (SIM) on test-en, and 1.29\% CER and 0.755 SIM on test-zh. GRPO improves these results to 1.15\% WER / 0.753 SIM and 1.17\% CER / 0.776 SIM, respectively. We also distill the guided flow head into two- and four-step students, requiring 2 or 4 head evaluations per coarse frame. The four-step student retains competitive SIM and nearly matches the supervised model in WER/CER.

### 9. PaperCritic: Training AI Reviewers to Assess the Soundness of Papers
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17834
Upvotes: 19

As AI conference submissions grow rapidly, AI reviewers are being considered as an alternative. However, simply imitating human reviewing is problematic because reviewing serves multiple purposes, including evaluating papers' soundness, interestingness, and clarity, the last two of which are subjective. In this work, we focus solely on evaluating soundness, the most important and objective criterion, and define the learning objective as jointly maximizing (1) precision, based on the soundness and substantiveness of review points, and (2) recall, the coverage of high-quality reference review points. Under this problem formulation, we construct a quality-labeled review point dataset, PaperSound-23K, using 1K ICLR submissions and 23K human-written and LLM-generated review points. Using this dataset, we train an AI reviewer model, PaperCritic, through reinforcement learning with the F1 score of precision and recall as the reward. Our experiments show that PaperCritic outperforms frontier models and models trained with other AI reviewer training methods on an in-domain test set. Surprisingly, learning to review ICLR submissions also transfers to reviewing Nature-family papers and even to out-of-domain reviewing tasks (e.g., code review, legal contract review, and feedback on story writing).

### 10. Accelerate Bench: Evaluating Agents for Application-Level Program Optimization
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20882
Upvotes: 14

Application optimization requires coding agents to discover useful changes and coordinate them across a complete program while preserving the required computation. We introduce Accelerate Bench, a benchmark of 32 CPU optimization tasks adapted from scientific applications and supercomputing competitions. Each task provides a working program and specifies the workload, correctness requirements, timing boundaries, and permitted edits, leaving agents to choose where and how to optimize. Public testing supports iterative development, and a separate verifier scores the final submission. On benchmark version 1.1, we evaluate 16 coding agents, each pairing a model with a harness. Their Score@1 ranges from 25.5% to 67.4%. Most of their programs are correct, and the size of the speedup separates them: the two leading agents are at least 2x faster on 22 or more of the 32 tasks, while the other fourteen reach 2x on 8 to 17 and leave 125 of their 412 correct outcomes below 1.25x. Controlled replays show why coordination across an application matters: identical source code runs between 0.29x and 2.12x depending only on build and runtime settings, and one code change is worth 1.01x or 6.4x depending on another. Accelerate Bench pairs executable workloads with agents' programs and trajectories to evaluate both their gains and the process behind them.

### 11. Calpha: Toward Native Biomolecular Intelligence in a General-Purpose Language Model
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20986
Upvotes: 11

Biomolecular foundation models predict the structures of proteins and their complexes accurately, and large language models (LLMs) reason in natural language, but the two remain separate: the abilities of the former are not part of a general-purpose LLM, and the reasoning of the latter does not enter a structure prediction. Calpha aims at one general model that folds proteins and complexes, understands protein sequence and structure, and lets its language reasoning influence the structures it predicts. This report covers the first step, in which an LLM outputs single-chain protein structures. Qwen3.5-9B reads a sequence enriched with ESM2 residue features. It can respond in text or emit a token that invokes a pretrained SimpleFold structure head, which receives information about the protein only through Qwen's residue states. Under a fixed folding request, the head achieves mean TM-scores of 0.808 on the FoldBench-334 development set, 0.635 on CASP16 domains and 0.900 on a CAMEO-style PDB set. We compare these predictions with a baseline in which ESM2-3B encodes the sequence and a fine-tuned SimpleFold-3B head predicts the coordinates. The two systems also differ in trainable weights, training duration and crop length. For chains of 100--299 residues, all three paired confidence intervals include zero. At 300--600 residues, the mean differences are -0.044, -0.160 and -0.025, respectively. In a separate test, four English request types triggered folding on every evaluated example. Protein training reduced accuracy on a 350-item MMLU-Pro subset from 77.4% to 70.3%. An LLM can therefore output single-chain structures from its own states, with lower long-chain scores in the current comparisons and reduced accuracy on the evaluated MMLU-Pro subset. Complex prediction, structure understanding and the effect of language reasoning on the predicted coordinates remain future work.

### 12. SALA: Semantically Aligned Latent Actions Enable Dual-Axis Scaling of Embodied Foundation Models
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17110
Upvotes: 10

Embodied foundation models hinge on diverse robot interaction data, which are costly to collect. Egocentric human videos offer a complementary source of experience, and latent action models (LAMs) provide a popular means of leveraging these videos for policy supervision. Compared with world-action models (WAMs), LAMs can offer greater training efficiency and potentially lower inference costs by avoiding explicit pixel prediction during policy learning and execution. However, most existing LAMs learn latent actions solely through video reconstruction, which can cause their representations to cluster by visual appearance rather than by the underlying actions. As a result, they struggle to align human actions with their robot counterparts. To bridge the gap, we introduce SALA, which emphasizes establishing the semantic correspondence for latent actions. Without requiring paired data, we use the language descriptions of unpaired human and robot clips to estimate semantic similarities and supervise cross-embodiment correspondence. Specifically, we translate these similarities into soft neighborhood structures that guide human latent actions toward semantically related robot latents, while robot action reconstruction grounds the latent space in control. The resulting shared latent action space provides a unified supervision signal for training an embodied foundation model on both human and robot videos. Notably, latent action supervision enables scaling along two axes: (1) pre-training on increasing amounts of general human video consistently reduces robot action prediction error on downstream tasks, and (2) post-training on increasing amounts of task-specific human video substantially improves real-world robot task success rates.

### 13. MovieRoll: A Systematic Framework for Rolling Out Long-Form Videos
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21339
Upvotes: 7

Streaming video generation must sustain quality beyond the training horizon while handling self-generated history and growing cache costs. To address these challenges, we present MovieRoll, a base-to-distilled framework that connects streaming fine-tuning, long-rollout distillation, and long-horizon inference. We first establish a robust multi-step base through Window-wise Teacher Forcing (WinTF), which jointly denoises chunks at progressive noise levels with bidirectional attention within each window and causal access to preceding history. To retain historical context efficiently, hierarchical KV-cache compression (HieraKV) exploits layer-wise attention patterns, retaining full history in shallow layers and sink--recent history in deeper layers. This reduces cache memory by up to 78% while improving generation quality. Building on this base, cumulative long tuning with distribution matching distillation (CuLT-DMD) holds parameters fixed throughout each long self-rollout and averages local gradients before updating. Each update therefore combines supervision across temporal positions while retaining only one local computation graph, enabling efficient long-rollout training of a few-step generator. To extend generation beyond the training horizon, KV recomputation bounds context for both models, while guidance annealing (AnnealCFG) reduces guidance toward the clean endpoint to stabilize MovieRoll-Base. Complementing these modeling advances, FBCA attention and PS-VAE decoding support efficient training and streaming output. On HeliosBench, MovieRoll-Base remains competitive at 30s after training on videos of up to 15s, and MovieRoll-Distilled achieves the highest total scores among the compared methods across single- and multi-event evaluations up to 60s, combining sustained naturalness with active motion.

### 14. Latent Feedback Is a Compute and Data Amplifier
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20404
Upvotes: 4

When high-quality data runs out, a common practice is to train on it for several epochs, but each repeat is worth less than the last. Latent feedback, which feeds each token's top-layer state into the next token's input, is another way to spend that compute, yet it has not been tested against a vanilla Transformer that spends the same compute on more epochs. We run this test across a ladder of model sizes, unique data and epochs, tuning both models at every size and amount of unique data. Latent feedback makes every FLOP and every token count for more except when unique data is scarce relative to model size, a regime that today's models are far from. We find that latent feedback serves as an amplifier: with unique data fixed, it reaches a Transformer's loss with less compute; with epochs fixed, it reaches that loss with less unique data. Furthermore, both savings grow with unique data per parameter and with repetition, and downstream tasks agree. To go beyond our grid, we fit one scaling law to both models, in which latent feedback enters as a Transformer with more parameters; it predicts that both savings keep growing with unique data and repetition. Fitted on models from 60M to 271M parameters, the law predicts the losses at a held-out 778M size to within 0.3% and the gap between the two models to within 0.05%. At most, latent feedback saves 48% of the compute and 47% of the unique data. When data, not compute, is the limit, latent feedback is the better use of a FLOP for a model of a given size.

### 15. LoopFill: Pipelined Multi-Token Speculative Decoding for Looped Transformers
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.19649
Upvotes: 3

Looped Transformers improve parameter efficiency by repeatedly applying the same Transformer layers. During autoregressive decoding, however, generating each token requires several sequential passes through these layers, leading to high decoding latency. Pipelined self-speculative methods use shallow-loop predictions to process tokens at different loops in the same batch, but each token must reach the drafting depth before the next can enter the pipeline. We introduce LoopFill, a training-free speculative decoding algorithm for looped LLM that combines multi-token suffix drafts with shallow-loop predictions in a single draft tree. Both drafters contribute to one tree, so a suffix lookup can immediately extend a shallow prediction and several future tokens can enter the pipeline together. The shared block computes the highest-scoring draft candidates at different loop depths in one batch, and full-depth verification preserves the model's autoregressive output distribution. Across seven benchmarks, LoopFill's average speedup over greedy autoregressive decoding is 5.85times on Ouro-2.6B and 5.13times on Huginn-3.5B. The speedups exceed those of LoopAhead, the strongest baseline, by \(40\%\) on Ouro-2.6B and \(25\%\) on Huginn-3.5B.

### 16. DominoBench: Evaluating Multi-Step Physical Reasoning from Still Images
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20385
Upvotes: 2

Can a vision-language model look at a picture of a chain reaction and tell what will happen? Recent multimodal models describe scenes fluently and solve textbook physics problems, which suggests they should also predict how a scene of ramps, ledges, pendulums, seesaws and dominoes plays out once a ball is pushed. We tested this claim on 30 models from 10 families and reached a clear conclusion: unaided, no model predicts the outcome reliably. We built DominoBench, a benchmark of still images of 2D rigid-body scenes whose outcome a physics simulator computes. It holds 854 items in 427 minimal pairs, in which a change of one to three grid steps in one quantity decides whether one person survives, and every item comes with a four-rung hint ladder ending in the exact calculation that decides the outcome. Unaided, frontier models from Anthropic, Google and OpenAI are right about the person whose fate flips in at most 72% of items and answer both twins of a pair correctly in at most 34%. Pointing at the deciding step helps little, and exact geometry given as text barely helps, yet once the deciding calculation and the quantities it needs, including speeds that a still image cannot show, are written out, the same models reach 82-92% on the deciding person, while small open-weight models stay near chance. Physical prediction from a still image therefore fails at the quantitative step: the strongest models use the deciding quantity when they are given it, but do not produce it on their own. For models that plan or act in the physical world, a fluent account of a scene is thus no evidence of a correct prediction: such models must find and compute the deciding quantity unprompted, and DominoBench tests whether they do.

### 17. UMM-JEPA: Generative Pretraining via Masked Next-Scale Representation Prediction for Unified Multimodal Models
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18668
Upvotes: 2

Unified multimodal models can support both visual understanding and generation, yet generative training does not necessarily improve understanding. We argue that this limitation may arise from a mismatch between the representation targeted by generation and the perceptual representation used for understanding. Conventional generation objectives primarily optimize synthesis-oriented spaces such as discrete visual codes or generative latents. We therefore introduce UMM-JEPA, which reformulates generation as progressive prediction of continuous visual-understanding representations. The model constructs a multi-scale representation hierarchy from coarse to fine, while pixel rendering is decoupled from representation pretraining and attached only afterward. Controlled comparisons show that the representation-prediction objective accounts for most of the understanding improvement, while a data-matched control leaves the group means nearly unchanged. UMM-JEPA reaches a 16-benchmark visual mean of 51.71, against 46.36 for the understanding-only Qwen-VL baseline, and the text mean also rises. Compared with discrete-code and latent-flow generation objectives, perceptual representation prediction yields stronger and more consistent transfer to understanding. Linear probes and visual-evidence interventions further reveal richer object and spatial structure and stronger use of image-specific information. These results suggest that generation can benefit understanding when it directly supervises the perceptual representations that understanding consumes.

### 19. GlyphAtlas: Disentangling Stroke Detail from Layout for Scene Text Synthesis
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20192
Upvotes: 1

Scene text synthesis requires stroke-accurate characters rendered in a specified layout, as well as visual coherence with the surrounding scene. To achieve this, existing methods commonly pre-render the target text at its intended locations to form a glyph image that jointly specifies layout and glyph structure. However, this representation ties the resolution of each glyph to its target region, limiting fine-grained stroke guidance for small, densely packed, or structurally complex text. To tackle this, we propose GlyphAtlas, a scene text synthesis framework that retains the glyph image as a spatial anchor and supplies fine-grained stroke references through a dedicated glyph atlas. The atlas renders and encodes each character or word in a separate cell, decoupling reference resolution from target-region size. We further introduce atlas-anchored attention, which lets each text region retrieve stroke detail only from its corresponding atlas cells to refine the coarse glyph structure provided by the glyph image. This coarse-to-fine conditioning simplifies stroke rendering while preserving explicit layout correspondence. We optimize GlyphAtlas with a mask-weighted flow matching objective and a timestep-weighted text perceptual loss for writing accuracy and background consistency. Random resolution augmentation of atlas cells supports adjustable atlas budgets at inference. Extensive experiments demonstrate that GlyphAtlas achieves stroke-accurate synthesis of small, dense, and structurally complex text, zero-shot transfer to languages unseen during task-specific training, and leading performance in scenes with many text regions. project page: https://fr0zencrane.github.io/project-glyphatlas/

### 20. Centered Ablation: Why Last-Layer Directional Ablation Under-Fires or Collapses, and a Closed-Form Fix
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.20148
Upvotes: 1

Directional ablation removes a hidden state's component along a "behaviour direction" while a language model runs. It is the standard way to switch a behaviour such as refusal off, but it is erratic: on some models it barely fires, on others the text collapses into repetition. We show why, and fix it. At the last residual stream, ablation is exactly a logit bias: a fixed vocabulary footprint scaled by a gate, the cosine between the hidden state and the direction. The gate is measured from the origin, but last-layer states share a large common mode far from it, so a direction that overlaps the common mode fires on benign text and under-fires on its targets. Centered Ablation (CA) projects the common mode out of the direction, in closed form. Across 8 models, CA raises attack success by 29 and 46 points where the standard recipe under-fires, cuts collapse on Gemma-2-9B from 50\% to 1\%, and preserves 12 to 40 points more benign answers on high-overlap models, 39 to 46 more than the affine offset of prior work. Two numbers computed before generation predict, on models the analysis had not seen, which gain faithfulness and which gain attack success, and a collapse index marks when text degenerates. We also show that two standard safety judges score collapsed text in opposite directions, which can fabricate a 46-point effect.

### 21. Continuation-Guided LLM Evolution of Program Policies and Goal Compilers in Craftax

### 22. OutfitterBench: Evaluating LLM Agents on Long-Horizon Resource Allocation and Commitment Coordination
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17933
Upvotes: 1

In many long-horizon settings, an agent's own choices create future constraints: committing finite capacity establishes future obligations and forecloses later opportunities. Sustaining performance therefore requires both keeping evolving plans feasible and comparing the value of resource allocations with the alternatives they displace. Recent benchmarks increasingly evaluate LLM agents in continuing operations, but give less attention to how agents keep their own commitments feasible while reassessing resource allocations as opportunities change. We introduce OutfitterBench, where agents manage an outdoor-equipment rental business for 200 days, purchasing from a 46-product catalog and handling roughly 1,100 reservation requests. Under a fixed capital ceiling, reservations and uncertain walk-in demand compete for reusable equipment whose availability changes over time. Experiments with seven frontier LLMs show that several agents underperform a simple rule-based baseline, while only the leading agent consistently surpasses it. Trajectory analysis shows that some agents apply known constraints inconsistently and make new decisions that conflict with existing commitments. Agents with more reliable fulfillment can still secure low returns from comparable commitments or delay resource reallocation. The leading agent better connects preparation with economic choices but also misses opportunities for more profitable asset use. These findings highlight limitations in sustaining coherent resource plans and reassessing their value over time. ### 23. A Reinforcement Learning Method to Navigate SMEFT Operator Space
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21439
Upvotes: 0

In particle physics, discrepancies between experimental measurements and Standard Model (SM) predictions are commonly interpreted within new theoretical models. In this context, the Standard Model Effective Field Theory (SMEFT) provides a general framework encompassing a broad class of models. However, exploring the enormous space of possible SMEFT operators rapidly becomes a challenging combinatorial problem: many operators can contribute simultaneously to different observables, their effects are correlated, and distinct operator combinations can provide comparably good descriptions of the data. In this work, we formulate the exploration of the SMEFT operator space as a sequential decision-making problem using reinforcement learning (RL). Our RL agent employs a lightweight actor-critic architecture with approximately 40K trainable parameters in total. Rather than searching for a single best-fit theory, our method is designed to discover a diverse set of high-quality theory hypotheses. We test the method in a complex phenomenological environment comprising 132 observables across several sectors of particle physics, including 24 measurements that deviate from their SM predictions by more than 1.5σ. We show that the learned RL policy efficiently constructs multi-operator theories that substantially improve the global fit. Comparisons with independent random-subset and random-sequential searches demonstrate the ability of the RL policy to navigate this large combinatorial space. Our results establish RL as a systematic framework for exploring diverse patterns of new physics (NP) interactions within SMEFT.

### 24. Reflection Anchors for Propagation-Aware Visual Retention in Long-Chain Multimodal Reasoning
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2605.09614
Upvotes: 0

Long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning improves large vision--language models, but visual information often fades during generation, limiting long-horizon multimodal reasoning. Existing methods either re-inject vision at inference or train policies for stronger grounding, but where to intervene relies on perception heuristics rather than principled gain analysis, and how local visual influence propagates remains implicit. We study this problem from an information-theoretic standpoint and derive a lower bound on the downstream visual gain of a one-step intervention, which suggests two factors: local branching room (token entropy) and downstream visual propagation potential (suffix divergence from a vision-marginalized reference). Guided by this analysis, we propose reflection-anchor policy optimization (RAPO), a GRPO-based policy optimization method that selects high-entropy reflection anchors and optimizes a chain-masked finite-window KL surrogate for downstream visual dependence. Experiments on reasoning-intensive and general-domain benchmarks show that RAPO delivers substantial gains over strong baselines across multiple LVLM backbones. Mechanism analyses further indicate that reflection anchors are enriched for visually sensitive decision points and that RAPO increases contrastive visual-dependence signals along generated trajectories.
