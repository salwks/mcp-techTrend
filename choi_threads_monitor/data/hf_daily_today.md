### 1. Motion Mamba 2.0: Autoregressive Diffusion Mamba for Long-Horizon Humanoid Control
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29897
Upvotes: 140

Long-horizon, real-time controllable motion generation is essential for interactive animation and humanoid robotics, where an agent must respond to changing instructions and spatial goals while moving. Existing autoregressive diffusion methods generate motion incrementally, but bounded attention caches limit access to distant history, while repeated denoising introduces substantial inference overhead. We present Motion Mamba 2.0, a Mamba-based root-body diffusion architecture that integrates local attention, resumable recurrent states, and persistent content memory (PCM) for long-horizon controllable motion generation. PCM transforms compressed motion history from Mamba recurrent states, together with state changes and text-conditioned features, into dynamically updated content slots accessible to local attention. To enable efficient inference, we further develop an execution scheme tailored to this architecture, combining CUDA Graph replay, fused kernels, and execution-flow simplification while preserving the canonical history update. Evaluations on retargeted HiPHI demonstrate accurate terminal control over rollouts of up to 39.52 s, while short-horizon comparisons on BONES Seed and retargeted HumanML3D show that the model also retains strong spatial control and text-motion matching. On an RTX 4090, the optimized execution reduces median complete-window latency from 1685.35 to 276.14 ms, achieving a median paired speedup of 6.138times while preserving identical outputs and committed states in the tested rollouts. Moreover, we conduct extensive real-world evaluation on the Unitree G1, demonstrating sequential text-guided action composition, expressive motion, and interactive locomotion through keyboard and mouse control.

### 2. Embo-Harness: Towards Understanding Frontier-Model-as-Policy Harness
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31201
Upvotes: 86

Frontier multimodal models exhibit extraordinary generalization in generating robot actions from observations and instructions, enabling a new paradigm in which they serve directly as policies within closed-loop robotic harnesses. However, the design principles of such harnesses, and the capabilities underlying their strong performance, remain unclear. We present Embo-Harness, a framework for systematically studying frontier-model-as-policy harnesses. We investigate four components of the robotic agentic loop, namely, prompting, planning, action execution, and tool use, and distill six empirical findings into a practical recipe. This recipe prioritizes interfaces that exploit the model's native visual reasoning and tool-use capabilities over handcrafted plans, skills, or action priors, and identifies effective choices for action representation, chunking, reasoning effort, visual observations, short multi-action sequences, and policy refinement. On RoboLab-120, Embo-Harness achieves an 83.3\% success rate, outperforming the strongest reported baseline by 26.2 percentage points. It also succeeds in 15 of 20 zero-shot trials across four real-world manipulation tasks. Beyond performance, controlled interventions indicate that the studied model depends more strongly on accurate spatial target estimation than on explicit low-level joint or trajectory generation. These results provide both a practical recipe for building strong frontier-model-as-policy harnesses and a clearer account of the capabilities that drive their performance.

### 3. 3AGameFactory-Motion: Agentic Rigging and Reliable Complex Motion Generation for Diverse 3D Assets
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30013
Upvotes: 56

Animating arbitrary 3D assets from instructions requires both structural inference and motion control, yet existing motion benchmarks mostly cover conventional human or animal skeletons with simple instructions. We introduce DivDynBench, which evaluates rigging and motion generation across six morphology categories, with instructions requiring multi-phase part coordination and explicit timing, angle, repetition, and trajectory constraints. With large language models, we systematically compare three common paradigms: specialized motion generation, video generation followed by motion recovery, and direct agent-driven generation. We analyze their control capabilities, cross-morphology adaptability, and generation costs. Guided by this comparison, we propose an Agentic Motion Framework in which an agent writes compact parametric skeleton and motion programs, and a deterministic executor performs rigging, kinematic solving, and geometric correction. Compact parameter control reduces generation overhead and improves rigging reliability and motion-control accuracy, providing practical guidance for deploying complex 3D motion generation.

### 4. WeMM-Ecom: Advancing Multimodal Content Understanding for WeChat E-Commerce
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30662
Upvotes: 52

We present WeMM-Ecom, a family of multimodal large language models that deliver frontier e-commerce capabilities while achieving state-of-the-art general-purpose performance at their respective scales. The family spans five sizes, from a 0.8B dense model to a 35B mixture-of-experts model with 3B active parameters. Our training pipeline begins with two-stage continued pre-training that progressively raises the proportion of e-commerce data within a diverse multimodal data mixture, followed by hybrid-thinking post-training that combines on-policy distillation and reinforcement learning to support both non-thinking and thinking modes. For evaluation, we construct a comprehensive suite of 28 e-commerce benchmarks (23 in-house and five open-source) covering eight business scenarios and complement it with 64 public general-purpose benchmarks. On the e-commerce suite, WeMM-Ecom-35B-A3B surpasses substantially larger open-source flagship models, including Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, HY4, and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. On the general-purpose suite, every WeMM-Ecom model surpasses its similarly sized Qwen3.5 counterpart, with the lightweight variants (e.g., WeMM-Ecom-2B) showing clear advantages over leading lightweight models such as Qwen3.5-2B, MiniCPM-V-4.7-1B, and LFM3-VL-2B. Beyond benchmarks, WeMM-Ecom now powers hundreds of production use cases in WeChat, including catalog management, multimodal product search, and livestream commerce, serving billion-scale requests per day. We publicly release all model weights and detail our data curation, training recipe, and benchmark construction to facilitate future work on domain-specialized multimodal models. We have released the model weights and code to facilitate future research at https://github.com/Tencent/WeMM-Ecom.

### 5. Let the Fixer Decide: Training a Code Localizer from the Fixer's Likelihood
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12164
Upvotes: 41

Code localizers are usually trained to predict the files a reference patch edits, but the code a fixer needs to read is not the same as the code it changes. We propose Fixer-Guided Localization (FGL): the localizer proposes several localizations, a frozen fixer scores each by how likely it makes a verified patch, and the localizer is trained on these scores with weighted supervised fine-tuning, without running tests and without reinforcement learning. We build the full pipeline on Gemma 4 E4B for the Kaggle Gemma-4 Developer Agent competition, from 1,629 test-verified Kimi K3 trajectories. The FGL target helps the fixer find the bug as often as the teacher's localization while using 27.5% less code. On the development set, one EM round moves the agent in the expected direction compared with a matched control: more tasks reach a patch (53 vs. 46), fewer tasks get stuck reading files that do not exist (43 vs. 54), and more tasks are solved (8 vs. 6). Training also changes how the agent works: trained agents explore about three times more than the base model and write far fewer wrong patches, and the single agent solves up to 11 of 78 tasks, against 8 for the base model. An analysis of 13 evaluation runs identifies what limits a 4B agent under an 8-minute budget (time, repetition loops and edit quality) and gives practical lessons for training small coding agents. All data, code and adapters are publicly available.

### 6. WorldLeague: Benchmarking Physical Agency
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30989
Upvotes: 40

AI systems can now operate Blender, follow instructions in complex video games, and manipulate objects with robot arms. What range of physical goals can today's AI systems achieve autonomously? Answering this question requires testing systems on tasks that demand different combinations of perception, memory, planning, and action. We introduce WorldLeague, a benchmark that compares AI systems on a common set of 60 simulated robot tasks by measuring progress toward each task's physical goal from the robot's trajectory. Robots explore scenes to gather evidence in WorldSeek, rearrange objects in WorldShape, and adapt to changes in WorldShift. Tying these goals to a single control interface limits which AI systems can be compared. In WorldLeague, vision-language models (VLMs) choose targets and motions, while vision-language-action models (VLAs) and world-action models (WAMs) use their native controls. For each of six capability dimensions, we measure how much progress a system makes toward its goals (Interactive Score) and how often it achieves them (physical success rate). Together, these measures form the system's Physical Agency Profile. Among 13 representative AI systems, Claude Fable 5.2 achieves 13 of the 60 goals and earns the highest mean Interactive Score (28.58/100). Fable leads in four dimensions, while GPT-6.1-Sol and GPT-6-Astra tie for first on rule-following tasks. Even when VLMs answer every final question correctly, they receive an Interactive Score of zero in 71.8\% of those episodes. WorldLeague gives Embodied AI, Physical AI, and Agentic AI a shared basis for comparing what AI systems achieve autonomously, from partial progress toward a physical goal to full goal completion. Project Webpage: https://xyangzhou.github.io/WorldLeague

### 7. A Glimpse of Training VLMs as Foundation Models for Robotic Manipulation
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29338
Upvotes: 35

Recently, GPT-6 Astra demonstrates strong generalization in robot control, yet limited disclosure of its training leaves open how such capabilities can be reproduced. We introduce nanoAstra, an approach to training vision-language models (VLMs) for generalizable manipulation that combines semantic action modeling with capability-preserving supervised fine-tuning (CP-SFT). nanoAstra represents robot behavior as semantic steps composed of reusable, parameterized action primitives, guided by explicit progress descriptions. Meanwhile, CP-SFT uses an explicit robot-specific system prompt to constrain manipulation outputs to a structured action space, aiming to focus adaptation on robot-specific contexts and promote specialization while minimizing interference with broader pretrained capabilities. With this formulation, we train nanoAstra on more than 5,000 hours of robot data together with general VQA data. On RoboTwin 2.0's randomized benchmark, nanoAstra-9B achieves a 17.8% zero-shot success rate, substantially outperforming advanced VLA and WAM baselines as well as similarly sized VLMs. With inference faster than the corresponding motion execution on a single H20 GPU, our model supports action-chunk-like streaming execution that overlaps inference with motion, reducing the robot's idle time from 35.0% to 6.9%.

### 8. ViDecide: Teaching Video Diffusion Models to Reason and Plan Step by Step
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26389
Upvotes: 30

Current leading video generation models can synthesize highly realistic videos, while still frequently violating physical and logical constraints in multi-step reasoning and planning, even after training with large-scale reasoning data or existing diffusion RL methods. We attribute these failures to the conventional video training paradigm, which treats a solution as a fixed-length clip rather than a chain of decisions. We present ViDecide, a decision-centric framework that trains video diffusion models to predict state transitions causally and decide when to stop, with a decision-focused training schedule. We further propose Corrective Fine-tuning (CFT), which corrects failed decisions by mapping their noise to the model's own successful continuations, achieving stable improvement without policy-gradient optimization. Across abstract games, planning tasks, and a new benchmark of multi-step robot planning, ViDecide achieves an average strict success rate of 83.8%, far above 23.3% for standard fine-tuning and 67.8% for the strongest prior method, and generalizes to larger puzzles and unseen layouts. Our framework therefore unlocks greater potential for video generation models as reliable reasoners and planners in the visual world. Project page: https://kevin-thu.github.io/ViDecide.

### 9. AutoPILOT: A Visual Agent Harness for Real-Time Gameplay
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28202
Upvotes: 28

Multimodal foundation models can play video games well when the game waits for them, but often fail when it keeps running. We find that much of this gap lies in the harness, which decides what the model sees, what it remembers, and how its outputs become actions over time. Existing harnesses such as VISTA improve the first two, yet turn each model call into at most one action, so the agent acts only as often as its model thinks. We introduce AutoPILOT, which changes the third: in each model call, the model can send a primitive action, start an open-loop macro, or write a closed-loop controller that keeps playing from the screen without further model calls until it hands control back. To evaluate agents in real-time games, we build AFK-Bench, 120 diverse browser games grouped by how they respond to inaction and playable in real time or with the clock paused. With GPT-6.1 Sol under the same budgets, AutoPILOT reaches 68.6% real-time success against 38.3% for VISTA, and on GameWorld it keeps 80.0% in real time while VISTA drops from 84.1% to 42.9%. Its gains hold across models, and it reaches VISTA's final success rate at a fifth of its API cost. Our results suggest that how a harness turns model outputs into actions over time is a central yet underexplored part of agent design, and we hope AutoPILOT and AFK-Bench will serve as a foundation for agents in a world that does not wait.

### 11. World Models as Native Latent Action Models
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30898
Upvotes: 22

Large and diverse video sources, such as egocentric human video and heterogeneous robot recordings, carry rich dynamics but come either without actions or without a shared action space. Latent action models (LAMs) can recover these dynamics, but they train their inverse- and forward-dynamics models (IDM, FDM) from scratch, trading off pixel reconstruction against latent capacity and accuracy. Pretrained video world models natively generate high-fidelity dynamics, yet represent them densely, entangled across all layers. Viewing the world model as a LAM, we show that these dynamics can be extracted with minimal changes to the model. Native LAM operates one pretrained video diffusion transformer as both IDM and FDM, with learnable dynamics registers as the only causal bridge between the two, and trains on counterfactual temporal pairs that differ in motion alone, so that the registers distill a video's dynamics into a compact native latent action. We further propose a pretraining paradigm for vision-language-action (VLA) policies, Pretraining Actions from World models (PAWlicy), in which Native LAM serves as a dynamics teacher on large-scale video; unlike world action models, it never generates video. Native latent actions outperform existing LAMs on the LARY benchmark in both action classification and control regression, and replay the same motion on new robots, in new scenes, and from human hands to robots, although no transfer objective is used in training. With a 3.8B model, PAWlicy reaches a 93.6% average success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 trained with domain-randomized demonstrations, the highest among VLAs. On RoboDojo, it is competitive on every dimension we evaluate, and its Precision, evaluated by us, is on par with the leading leaderboard entries. On real robots, it improves over the pi_0.5 baseline on both tasks. Both stages use only public data and a publicly released world model.

### 12. RUIBench: What Makes a Good Robot-Use Interface?
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31115
Upvotes: 20

Foundation models can reason about complex physical tasks, yet translating this intelligence into reliable robot execution remains challenging. We argue that a critical, often overlooked factor is the robot-use interface (RUI), which determines how agents express and execute physical actions. To systematically study its impact, we introduce RUIBench, comprising 30 manipulation tasks across five suites spanning world knowledge, in-context learning, discovery, dynamic interaction, and contact-rich manipulation. We further develop RUIKit, a unified framework that crosses six action abstractions with two interaction forms, yielding 13 interfaces under shared perception and control. In simulation, changing only the RUI shifts a frontier agent's success rate from 45% to 81%, and that of a fine-tuned 4B model from 13% to 47%. Our analysis reveals that effective interfaces reduce unnecessary decisions while preserving essential feedback, with their benefits shaped by agent behavior and physical uncertainty. We further uncover a verification gap, where agents frequently declare success despite observable failures, revealing the need for task-level verification beyond individual actions. Together, these findings establish RUI design as a critical factor in embodied agent reliability.

### 13. ViDiHand2: Unifying Video Diffusion Priors and Hand Geometry for 3D Hand Motion Reconstruction in the Wild
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30597
Upvotes: 19

Learning robot manipulation from egocentric human videos requires accurate metric 3D hand motion. Recent methods leverage pretrained video diffusion model (VDM) priors to recover occluded hands directly from full-frame videos, but their appearance-based depth regression generalizes poorly to unseen cameras. In this work, we propose ViDiHand2, a geometry-driven framework that reformulates VDM-based hand depth estimation from direct regression into fine-grained geometric constraint solving. Specifically, we introduce Fine-Grained Geometric Lifting and Clip-Level Ray Fitting, which recover metric depth by enforcing geometric consistency between pixel-aligned 2D joints and the metric structure of MANO hands, while leveraging temporal observations across the clip. Since the lifting relies on these 2D joints, to improve robustness under occlusion and image truncation, we further develop Confidence-Gated Fusion to adaptively combine geometrically lifted joints with MANO predictions according to localization confidence. Finally, we enhance the VDM backbone to better capture spatial-temporal representations and produce the per-frame 2D joint and MANO predictions that both steps rely on. Extensive experiments on seven public datasets demonstrate that ViDiHand2 consistently outperforms 18 baselines, reducing in-the-wild absolute joint error by 42% and detection failures by 8.82x compared with the best baseline on each metric, while running at 57 FPS. The code is available at https://github.com/NTUYWANG103/ViDiHand.

### 15. DiLE: Discrimination-Aware Latent-Space Learning for Universal Multimodal Embeddings
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25419
Upvotes: 16

Multimodal large language models have greatly improved input understanding in universal multimodal embedding models. However, this understanding does not ensure that their embeddings preserve the evidence needed to distinguish relevant items from confusable alternatives. To preserve essential discriminative evidence in embeddings, this paper proposes DiLE, a discrimination-aware latent-space learning framework that combines task guidance with on-policy retrieval feedback. Specifically, a few learnable queries are used to encode task-relevant information in a compact latent space. Reasoning instructions and auxiliary answer-generation supervision shape these representations to support both task understanding and retrieval discrimination. To address task-specific discriminative needs, we design shared-private expert adaptation that integrates cross-task knowledge sharing with task-specific representation learning. Building on this, the on-policy hard-negative distillation is introduced: the student retrieves confusable candidates, and a reverse KL divergence objective aligns its embedding-based similarity distribution with the teacher's fine-grained relevance distribution over these candidates. Candidates are periodically refreshed using updated student snapshots, tying teacher supervision to the student's evolving retrieval behavior and confusions. Across four model configurations that span two backbone generations, DiLE achieves state-of-the-art performance on MMEB-v2, UVRB, and MRMR, demonstrating its effectiveness across modalities and tasks.

### 16. Polytropos: What Do Vision-Language Model Descriptions Make of a Cultural Object?
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29355
Upvotes: 14

Vision-language models have become a new kind of describer, writing about almost anything people see, and their descriptions now shape how cultures are known by people and by other models. Descriptions of cultures tend to turn particular things into types, and what a description loses shows what its describer lacks. We introduce Polytropos, a benchmark that measures what model descriptions of cultural objects keep of the particular object by turning each description back into an image and comparing it with the original photograph. On 374 objects from the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Getty, with 1,870 descriptions from five models and 230 written by curators, reconstructions are recognised as the recorded culture more often than the photographs, at 54.55% to 59.09% against 52.14%, yet 16% to 21% of objects change outcome and only 0.49% of reconstructions are identified as the specific object. On the same objects, models lose recorded cultures at a rate similar to curators but gain more. Model descriptions give each object many faces, each typical of its culture and almost none the object itself.

### 17. Open Vision Banana: An Open Framework for Unified Visual Perception through Generation in RGB Space
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28489
Upvotes: 14

Vision Banana showed that a closed image generator, instruction-tuned to paint perception outputs as RGB images, becomes a generalist vision model. Does this carry over to open generators, and if not, what fails? We answer with Open Vision Banana (OVB), an open framework that adapts the 7B diffusion generator of Qwen-Image-2.1 into one instruction-driven model for metric depth, semantic, instance, and referring segmentation, using a single shared low-rank adapter and public data. Each task is a task contract whose RGB encoder, instruction, and decoder share one specification and are checked before training. Studying 42 externally evaluated checkpoints from two 100k-update runs, six exact weight-space merges, and eight controlled shorter runs, we find that the gap to the closed system is specific and measurable. The scale is not committed by a single sample: a ten-seed ensemble lifts unaligned δ_1 from 0.348 to 0.516 without retraining. Depth and 2D segmentation compete for the shared adapter, and weight merging moves along this trade-off without escaping it. Finally, lower validation flow loss comes with lower Cityscapes accuracy (Spearman ρapprox-0.9 between loss reduction and mIoU), so the loss cannot select checkpoints. We release code, contracts, training data, and all evaluation outputs.

### 18. UUBench: Understanding the Unspoken toward Full-Duplex Multimodal Interaction
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30157
Upvotes: 13

Effective real-time, full-duplex multimodal interaction requires systems to understand not only what users say, but also the communicative needs conveyed through prosody, facial expressions, gestures, and context, and to generate audiovisual responses that appropriately address those needs. Existing evaluations offer limited insight into whether systems translate an understanding of such unspoken needs into effective responses. We introduce UUBench, a benchmark for assessing how well multimodal interactive systems Understand the Unspoken intent and address users' implicit communicative needs. The inherent subjectivity of implicit intent inference poses a key challenge for evaluation. To address this, we introduce Process-Oriented Scoring, which grounds the assessment of intent fulfillment in observable auditory and visual cues, such as prosody and gestures. Rather than relying solely on a single overall score, it evaluates how well a response addresses the communicative needs conveyed by each applicable primitive dimension. We aggregate the dimension-level scores according to each dimension's relevance to the annotated evidence. Beyond intent fulfillment, Outcome-Oriented Scoring evaluates the naturalness, appropriateness, and human-likeness of the reply itself under audio-only, visual-only, and audiovisual conditions. To make these judgments traceable to observable evidence, we propose a Fact-Grounded Scoring JEV module, which uses structured, timestamped audiovisual observations of the user and the digital human's reply to assess one dimension or criterion at a time. UUBench provides a foundation for assessing whether multimodal interactive systems respond effectively to meaning beyond literal wording, supporting progress toward context-aware, real-time, full-duplex interaction.

### 20. Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation of RL Experts: Toward Continual Post-Training
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27376
Upvotes: 12

RL post-training typically produces domain experts that must then be consolidated into one model, and as new experts keep arriving, this consolidation becomes a continual process. Weight merging and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) must re-consolidate the whole model whenever an expert is added, and can erode capabilities outside the target domains. We propose Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation (CSPD), which keeps the pre-RL model frozen, distills each RL expert independently into a soft prompt, and composes experts by concatenating their prompts without joint training. A single soft prompt suffices to distill an RL expert, recovering at least 90% of the teacher's gain in 22 of 23 math, code, and agent settings. Independently trained prompts compose well. In a multi-expert setting, the average rises with every expert added, and the full composition matches MOPD (67.83 vs. 67.79). The composition generalizes best across six held-out benchmarks. Input-space adaptation adds little serving overhead, keeping throughput within 1.5% of the unadapted backbone and reaching 2.40x that of rank-4 LoRA. These results point toward continual post-training on a fixed backbone.

### 21. HIVE-Bench: Evaluating Patch-Level Visual Representations for Egocentric Robot Manipulation
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30813

Pretrained vision is a common start for robot learning, but most comparisons still score one pooled vector from a third-person image. ### 22. WeightCode: Installing New Capabilities into Neural Networks via Weight-Space Programming
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25476
Upvotes: 10

Neural networks encode learned capabilities in their weights, yet extending those capabilities with explicit task knowledge remains a fundamental challenge. Here we show that Weight-Space Programming (WSP), in which executable programs read, construct or modify a network's weights directly, can install new capabilities within trained neural networks. WeightCode, a WSP framework, uses a frozen language model to write executable weight programs that install new computations into trained neural networks using task knowledge and their existing weights, without gradient-based training. Once installed, the new capabilities run independently of the language model. Given a grammar or eight demonstrations, weight programs enable previously unseen command compositions (0% to 99.8% exact match) and a new pickup action (0% to 98.4% success), while preserving the original command and navigation capabilities. On ImageNet, four weight programs achieve net gains of 8.84-17.62 points while preserving clean accuracy, outperforming the best of eight fine-tuning methods under matched examples, checks, and retention constraints. In a Vision Transformer, causal interventions show that installed noise-correction gates drive robustness gains, while their selective activation preserves clean accuracy. Rerun on five independently trained Vision Transformers without further language model calls, ten weight programs retain 83-110% of their source-network gains on average, whereas copying their fixed weight edits yields negligible gains. Six programs written for an already edited network add 5.23-14.50 points of contrast robustness while retaining the previously installed noise gain. Together, these results establish WSP as a new route to acquiring and accumulating capabilities in trained neural networks through reusable weight programs.

### 23. Self-WSP: Can Language Models Improve Themselves Through Weight-Space Programming?
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28833
Upvotes: 9

Frontier language models can now read and edit the weights of a neural network much like code and thereby improve a small model, an approach known as Weight-Space Programming (WSP). We further explore applying WSP to a language model itself, which we call Self-WSP. Specifically, Qwen3.5-0.8B reads its own weights, proposes an edit, and then continues with the edited weights as both problem solver and editor. We find many cases of genuine self-improvement. Self-edits can raise GSM8K accuracy from 11.8% to 62.2% and MATH500 accuracy from 7.0% to 32.2%, sometimes by changing only 16 parameters, and in some cases repeated self-editing keeps improving the model. A closer look, however, reveals that these gains lack interpretability. The model shows little understanding of the edits it makes, and WSP driven by random search sometimes performs comparably. Self-WSP thus holds promise as a path toward recursive self-improvement (RSI), but realizing this promise requires further exploration.

### 51. Seeing Right, Writing Wrong: Where Short Chain-of-Thought Breaks Spatial VQA
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28665
Upvotes: 0

Asking a vision-language model (VLM) to think step by step often makes it worse at spatial questions, even though the image stays in context. We show that short chain-of-thought (CoT) turns a perception problem into a transcription problem. Once a VLM writes a spatial relation, its answer follows that sentence instead of the image, so whether CoT helps or hurts depends largely on whether the written relation is more accurate than answering directly. Studying 15 open VLMs (2B-38B) and six frontier closed models, we rule out token count, turn format, and visual forgetting in open models and trace the failure through three stages. First, writing re-weights visual cues, weakening a "lower is closer" shortcut in all 13 open models tested but also discarding valid cues, which produces false relations. Second, the written text takes over. Frontier closed models are not hurt: they write relations as accurately as they answer, and three of six re-check the image after writing. The three queried with reasoning off lock in like open models, so re-grounding co-occurs with answer-turn reasoning; we identify answer-step re-grounding as the step open VLMs lack. ### 52. LLMs Memorize Their Own Hallucinations During Post-Training
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26863

Reasoning domains have shaped the post-training recipes of frontier models even though they are trained and deployed in domains beyond math and code. In this paper, we study the consequences of directly adopting reasoning-centric training recipes, e.g., the use of outcome-based rewards for reinforcement learning (RL), injection of "cognitive" behaviors such as proposing and backtracking from hypotheses in reasoning chains, etc., for knowledge tasks. To study this, we train models with standard post-training, extract their self-generated hallucinations during RL, and track memorization throughout the rest of training. We find that models' recall of hallucinations reinforced during training increases by 4.83% on average, compared to just 0.17% for self-hallucinations that receive no training signal. Overall, our work identifies and systematically studies a previously unexamined pathway through which models learn hallucinations during training.

### 54. Deterministic, Reproducible Inference in Exact Integer Arithmetic, and Verifiable Distributed Inference as Its Consequence
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13730
Upvotes: 0

Optimized floating-point inference is not reproducible across environments: the same model on different hardware, compilers or thread counts can produce different bits. We present tritify, an open-source runtime that executes the forward pass of real models -- decoder-only language models, Mixture-of-Experts decoders, and the Segment Anything 2.1 image/video stack -- with the entire run-time path in exact integer arithmetic; floating point is confined to offline quantization and the float reference used for verification. We make three separate claims and verify each. (1) Cross-hardware reproducibility: the integer path produces bit-identical output run to run, across CPU instruction-set architectures, and across CPU/GPU, carried by a reproducing SHA-256 of the output. (2) Decision fidelity: the integer path reproduces the float reference's decision on the models and inputs tested, with the numeric exceptions measured; per-layer fidelity through sixteen layers of OLMoE-1B-7B and of a 106-billion-parameter MoE shows no fidelity cliff. (3) Location independence: a model's computation can be split across instruction sets, CPU and GPU, and networked machines with zero effect on the output bits. GLM-4.5-Air (106,852,251,264 parameters, 427 GB of integer codes) generated its answer across three machines, none of which can hold its integer codes in RAM, bit-identically to a single-host reference. The consequence is verifiable distributed inference: an untrusted server or a fleet of devices runs the model while an open scalar oracle re-derives the exact bytes; a guarantee against a dishonest server is future work.
