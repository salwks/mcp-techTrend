### 1. Wave Forcing: Towards Speed-of-Light Streaming Video Generation
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00859
Upvotes: 80

Real-time long-video generation requires continuous output with consistent appearance and natural motion. Existing chunk-wise autoregressive methods face challenges in both generation efficiency and long-video quality: serialized denoising and history-cache refresh require two forward passes even with one-step denoising, and longer rollouts can degrade quality and cause camera drift. We propose Wave Forcing (WF), which co-designs generation, training, and execution for continuous video output with multi-step denoising. WF uses chunk-wise causal attention to access earlier, cleaner chunks before their denoising is complete, and distributes staggered denoising and cache-refresh stages across full-model replicas to achieve 1F1C: one output chunk per concurrent forward round after pipeline fill. To adapt the model to this continuous generation process, we develop a three-stage training procedure that successively introduces mixed-noise denoising, motion supervision, and causal adaptation using self-generated history. To execute this process efficiently, Wave Runtime (WaveRT) uses Wave Parallelism (WP) for early K/V delivery and communication-computation overlap, together with optimized kernels and pipelined VAE decoding. Experiments on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B show that 5-step WF maintains overall quality comparable to Rolling Forcing while achieving higher Dynamic Degree on both short and long videos. Our system efficiency experiments show that 4-step WF achieves 147.6 DiT FPS and 134.8 end-to-end FPS on eight H200 GPUs, yielding 7.07x the single-GPU end-to-end throughput. In DiT-only tests with matched SageAttention+W8A8 backends, denoising steps, and GPU counts, WP delivers up to 2.53x the throughput of the faster of the Ulysses and Ring context-parallel baselines across six model scales (1.3B-14B).

### 2. Video Generation Meets Agent Harness: Agentic Video Generation, Agentic World Modeling and Recursive Self-Improvement
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40191
Upvotes: 58

Video generation is shifting from end-to-end synthesis toward agentic generation, where planning, tool use, and feedback enable coherent, controllable multi-shot videos. In parallel, video world models provide action-conditioned observations and predicted futures, serving as interactive environments for agents. We study their intersection through the agent harness: an executable runtime that constructs context, exposes tools, maintains state, routes feedback, and manages budgets around video models. This report examines three directions. First, agentic video generation, where harnesses coordinate scripting, shot planning, asset management, production, and editing, alongside the evaluation challenges of these systems. Second, agentic world modeling, where harnesses mediate observation, control, execution, and state. We discuss hybrid designs in which game engines or agent-written programs maintain world state while video models render observations, and how agents and world models can mutually evaluate and improve one another. Third, recursive self-improvement (RSI) in video model development, distinguishing output revisions, updates to harness state, generator parameters, and evaluators, and changes to the improvement procedure itself. We identify how these improvements feed back into subsequent development. Across these directions, we analyze essential agent capabilities, including audiovisual perception, long-horizon planning, memory, code-based interaction, feedback-guided control, skill reuse, and multi-agent coordination. We conclude with challenges in autonomous video production, lifelong memory, programmable hybrid control, autonomous model development, provenance, cost accounting, and evaluation linking video quality and task performance to sustained improvements in models and harnesses.

### 3. CoworkOS:Scaling Agent Coworkers for Real-world Automation
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03178
Upvotes: 56

Rapid advances in cowork agents are shifting human-AI collaboration toward real-world automation, delivering software, designs, and other artifacts. However, such productivity work demands both high-quality delivery on complex productivity tasks and continued monitoring and decision-making over long horizons, a combination that remains an open challenge. We introduce CoworkOS, a general-purpose harness for long-horizon work that separates task strategy from execution management: the model decides actions, delegation, and acceptance, while the runtime maintains state and feedback. Its three-layer architecture enables direct execution or delegation to Subagents, synchronously or asynchronously, under stable responsibilities. Through a shared application layer, humans and agents inspect and revise the same evolving documents, presentations, CAD models, and Blender scenes, so human-in-the-loop participation can enter both through conversation and through direct modification of the work itself. To sustain continuity across agents and sessions, CoworkOS further introduces hierarchical workspaces and persistent semantic memory, so working files, key decisions, and long-running Job logs remain shareable, reusable, and resumable after interruption. Across 12 benchmarks and 6 categories of real-world applications, CoworkOS proves highly competitive with frontier agent applications such as Codex and DeepSeek Harness. As a sustained-work case study, CoworkOS uses GLM-5.3 as its backbone to conduct RSI-driven automated post-training on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. Over five rounds, the model's average score across eight benchmarks rose from 33.19 to 42.95 (+9.76). Looking ahead, we believe CoworkOS opens a meaningful path toward general cowork-agent intelligence.

### 4. CUA-Master: Scaling Computer-use Agents through Open-World Recursive Improvements
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03126
Upvotes: 37

Despite recent advances in CUAs, their development remains constrained by the limited diversity, controllability, and reliability of interaction data. Moreover, conventional optimization pipelines lack iterative feedback, while existing recursive improvement approaches often rely on self-generated experiences, limiting ability expansion and yielding diminishing returns. To address these challenges, we introduce CUA-Master, a nested double-loop recursive improvement framework. At the system level, an outer world-data-model flywheel continuously incorporates open-world corpus and leverages evolving model weaknesses to guide subsequent data construction and optimization iterations. At the data level, an inner flywheel couples open-world seeding and autocatalytic propagation. The former mines authentic workflows as seed tasks from instructional videos, and employs visual-semantic dual-path trajectory reconstruction; The latter transforms these seeds into reusable primitives for controllable synthesis of diverse, complex, and verifiable tasks. To enable efficient and reliable trajectory collection, we further introduce a feedback-guided snapshot-rollback retry mechanism in various real environments. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CUA-Master-27B achieves competitive performance across GUI-only and GUI-CLI hybrid benchmarks, scoring 80.2 on OSWorld Verified, 36.9 on OSWorld 2.0, 43.9 on WeaveBench, and 25.0 on Agents' Last Exam. Our framework also substantially improves the quality and efficiency of data synthesis, and exhibits continuous gains across recursive iterations.

### 6. NorthCUA Technical Report: Turbocharging Computer-Use Agents through Experience at Scale
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02102

Scaling computer-use agents from individual actions to complex software work requires experience that connects decisions to complete outcomes. We present NorthCUA, an integrated system for turbocharging computer-use agents through experience at scale. Its Experience Flywheel supplies diverse executable tasks to the NorthCUA Harness, which sustains long interactions, records decisions, and checks task outcomes. Together, these advances establish NorthCUA as a high-performing model family and a reusable foundation for agents that carry complex software work from intent to completion.

### 7. Reviso: Not All Visual Evidence Deserves Equal Attention in Video Captioning
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03602
Upvotes: 23

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can generate detailed video captions, yet they may overemphasize minor details while overlooking essential context and key events. We argue that not all visual evidence deserves equal attention, as the importance of visual information depends on the content of each video. Motivated by this observation, we propose Reviso, an agentic video captioning framework that learns what to observe and how to gather relevant visual evidence. From a low-frame-rate global preview, Reviso constructs a hierarchical Context--Focus--Points (CFP) observation plan that identifies the video context, prioritizes key aspects, and defines concrete observation targets and corresponding tools. Guided by this plan, Reviso performs goal-directed active perception by selectively revisiting temporal segments and combining tools for fine-grained inspection. It interprets the acquired evidence, updates its plan as needed, and integrates global and local information to generate the final caption. To learn this observation strategy, we develop a verification-guided pipeline that constructs high-quality training trajectories through factual verification and teacher-guided refinement. We further train Reviso using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), incorporating a key-fact reward for caption quality, a Plan Alignment Reward (PAR) for identifying important observation targets, and an Unproductive Tool-Call Penalty (UTCP) that adjusts observation-cost penalties based on caption quality. Experiments across four video captioning benchmarks show that Reviso outperforms the evaluated open-source baselines, while its generated captions better support downstream video question answering. These findings highlight the effectiveness and potential of priority-guided active observation for video captioning.

### 8. Lost in Recovery: Demystifying Recovery Behavior of Computer-Use Agents via Goal-Oriented Action Replay
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27954
Upvotes: 22

Recent advances in large (vision) language models have enabled computer-use agents that automate digital workflows via fine-grained keyboard and mouse operations. However, such agents remain fragile in long-horizon settings, where errors accumulate and executed actions leave unintended side effects, making the ability to recover from failures critical. Yet, existing remedies remain inadequate: environment resets are infeasible in proprietary settings, action replay is prohibitively expensive over long horizons, and API-based rollback is limited to web environments, leaving model-based recovery largely unexplored. To address this gap, we present ReTrace, a scalable evaluation framework for systematically studying recovery behavior in computer-use agents. We formalize Transition-Consistent Recovery: identical downstream actions from the recovered and target states must yield identical outcomes. We operationalize this criterion through goal-oriented action replay following controlled error injection. Using ReTrace, we present the first systematic study of autonomous, model-based recovery, revealing that (i) ideal recovery can unlock model's latent task completion capabilities, (ii) current computer-use agents fail to exploit their latent recovery capabilities, and (iii) accurate model-based recovery yields measurable end-to-end gains. Together, these findings establish recovery as a fundamental capability for building reliable computer-use agents. Our code and dataset is available at https://github.com/BangProx/ReTRACE.

### 9. From Fragmentation to Fusion: A Survey and Roadmap for Omnimodal Embeddings
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00172
Upvotes: 20

Embedding models underpin virtually every modern AI system, from search engines to recommendation systems. Despite shared mathematical foundations, research on embedding models remains fragmented by modality: the communities develop largely in isolation, seldom exchanging insights. We argue that this fragmentation obscures a deeper convergence. Driven by contrastive learning, Transformer architectures, and a granularity shift, embedding research across modalities is converging toward a common set of principles, even as modality-specific challenges remain underexplored. To reason across this convergence without conflating it, we propose a framework of three progressively harder capabilities: coverage, comparability, and compositionality. Three paths toward unification satisfy these in turn: transformation broadens coverage, alignment enables comparability, and joint embedding, the terminus we advocate, aims at compositionality. This position paper traces the resulting evolution, providing the first unified, cross-cutting treatment of omnimodal embedding research.

### 12. Diagnosing the Reference Gap: What Visual Scaffolds Can and Cannot Fix in Multimodal Reasoning
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39419
Upvotes: 14

Multimodal large language models can recognize visual content yet struggle to reason reliably over it across multiple steps. In counting, a model may see all objects but lose track of which ones it has counted. We argue that such failures, commonly attributed to perception, reveal a reference gap: the failure to bind language to the relevant visual entities, regions, or locations. Motivated by the theory of visual routines, we test whether external references such as numbering can narrow this gap. We introduce ReGap-Bench, a paired benchmark of five visual reasoning tasks in which the scene, question, and answer are fixed and only the reference varies. Results show that task-matched references improve accuracy by up to 25\%, while mismatched references provide little benefit or even hurt performance. These findings identify the reference gap as a bottleneck distinct from visual perception and show that constructing task-appropriate visual references can serve as a training-free visual skill for improving multimodal reasoning.

### 13. DecisionRank: On-Policy Distillation for Decision-Model Rerankers
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.42128
Upvotes: 9

Decision models target structured tasks by directly predicting choices or scores, avoiding autoregressive decoding latency. However, transferring a generative LLM's ranking knowledge to a decision model remains challenging: the teacher expresses preferences through a ranked list of document ID sequences, while the student determines the ranking using scores from the first-token position. To bridge this gap, we introduce DecisionRank, a decision model trained through item-space on-policy distillation (OPD). Item-space OPD constructs the student's rollout by sorting its document scores into a ranked sequence. A generative teacher takes the resulting ranking list as inputs and provides conditional feedback to directly supervise those scores. Combined with offline ranking supervision, DecisionRank achieves state-of-the-art performance on both general and reasoning-intensive retrieval benchmarks such as TREC DL, BEIR, and BRIGHT. Across four benchmarks, item-space OPD consistently outperforms alternative training objectives. Even at 1.7B parameters, DecisionRank surpasses its 27B generative teacher on TREC DL19--23. At 8B, it outperforms its generative listwise counterpart while running 24.5times faster on TREC DL and 16.7times faster on BRIGHT.

### 14. DeFT-RAG: Decomposing Multi-Hop Questions into Self-Contained Sub-Questions with Reusable Answers
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37826
Upvotes: 7

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) lets a large language model (LLM) answer questions by retrieving relevant passages from a large corpus. However, RAG struggles with multi-hop questions, which require chaining facts spread across several documents through intermediate entities that the question never mentions. Graph-based RAG methods retrieve once from the entities a question names and so rarely recover the full evidence chain. Iterative RAG methods recover it step by step, but their accuracy is limited by the many tasks they ask the LLM to perform, such as planning the sub-questions, deciding their dependencies, rewriting them as answers arrive, and keeping track of what is known. We propose DeFT-RAG, a RAG framework built on three components. First, DeFT-RAG generates one self-contained sub-question at a time from the original question and the sub-questions answered so far with their answers (QA pairs). Each sub-question names every entity it asks about, which removes the need to plan, track dependencies or rewrite, and lets the LLM focus on a single fine-grained task at a time. Second, DeFT-RAG moves the remaining LLM work outside the LLM: rule-based updates to a QA table that keeps track of what is known, and yes/no decisions made by a fine-tuned lightweight decision model. Third, DeFT-RAG caches the QA pairs and reuses an answer when a later question asks a sub-question with the same meaning. On MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA and HotpotQA, DeFT-RAG achieves the best accuracy among thirteen baselines with both gpt-4o-mini and Qwen3.6 as the backbone, while giving the LLM fewer input tokens than any other iterative method. With a QA cache shared across 1,000 MuSiQue questions, 23.1% of sub-questions are answered from the cache, which reduces LLM calls by 12.9% without lowering accuracy.

### 15. FLAMIA: FLAsh MIcroscaling Attention for Fast and Causally Safe Training on Blackwell
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03344
Upvotes: 6

Attention remains a substantial computational cost in Transformer training, particularly at long context lengths. NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs provide high-throughput low-precision matrix multiplication, but exploiting it in attention requires addressing quantization overhead, causal information leakage through shared scales, and numerical inconsistencies between the forward and backward passes. We present FLAMIA, a family of attention kernels for Blackwell with MXFP8 and mixed NVFP4/FP8 training paths. Our design combines token-block scaling that reuses quantized operands across matrix orientations, targeted safeguards against scale-induced leakage, and consistent forward and backward computations. Fused quantization and architecture-specific kernel optimizations reduce overhead, while document-local scaling and workload-aware scheduling support variable-length documents directly in packed layouts without repacking. Across various causal packed-document workloads, the MXFP8 path accelerates the combined forward and backward attention call, including quantization, by factors of 1.10--1.40times on B200 and 1.06--1.42times on B300 relative to the fastest evaluated baseline for each workload. We validate training stability and quality preservation on pretraining Llama-style models. These results show how jointly designing quantization, training numerics, and document scheduling translates low-bit hardware throughput into practical attention acceleration.

### 16. BYOD: Build Your Own DLM - Efficient Autoregressive-to-Diffusion Language Model Conversion
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41574
Upvotes: 5

Unlike autoregressive (AR) models, diffusion language models (DLMs) permit parallel token updates, flexible generation, and bidirectional attention, but leading systems rely on costly pretraining or full-model fine-tuning. We present Build Your Own DLM (BYOD), a generalizable LoRA-only recipe for parameter-efficient AR-to-DLM conversion. Without architecture-specific tuning, BYOD induces diffusion generation in Gemma 2 9B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen2.5 7B, and Ministral 8B; to our knowledge, this is the first reported AR-to-DLM conversion of a Mistral model. The adapters comprise 4.1-5.8% of final parameters while every base-model parameter remains frozen; each 25,000-iteration conversion needs at most 102.4M tokens, processed in 3.1-4.0 hours on one Google Colab GPU. All four models generate from fully masked sequences, use bidirectional context in a controlled attention test, and retain substantial parent-model knowledge. BYOD-Gemma is the strongest BYOD conversion on our nine-task evaluation, reaching 50.6% average accuracy compared with 51.9% for LLaDA 8B Instruct in the same harness. Open-generation quality at low network function evaluation (NFE) budgets remains the principal limitation: BYOD perplexity is 8.3-11.1 versus 4.1 for LLaDA and 2.2-3.0 for the AR parents when generating 128 tokens in 64 steps. To enable further experimentation and inspection, we provide a standalone training and evaluation implementation and interactive demos for all four conversions. BYOD thus provides a practical route for extending diffusion generation to other existing model families while identifying the gap that efficient conversion must next close.

### 18. MAServeBench: Benchmarking Multi-Agent LLM Serving Systems with Hierarchical Collaboration Workloads

### 20. Measuring AI Agents as Teammates with Collaborative Intelligence
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.02633
Upvotes: 3

Progress in AI is often measured by what a single model can do on its own. Yet teams of AI agents are starting to make breakthroughs and discoveries in science, medicine, mathematics and software engineering. As AI begins to work in teams of agents and people, the ability to collaborate becomes a core part of their intelligence. Research on human teams has measured this ability for decades. Inspired by this, we introduce the Collaborative Intelligence Benchmark, which measures how well an AI agent works as a teammate. It contains 240 tasks across 8 dimensions of teamwork, from refusing an unsafe shortcut to checking the work that a weaker model leaves behind. Each task comes from a real record or an existing benchmark, from aviation accident reports and clinical cases to coding tasks and negotiations, and has a programmatically verifiable outcome. To isolate teamwork from individual capability, models are scored only on tasks that they can solve by themselves when they have full information. We evaluate 18 closed- and open-weight models and find that every model, across sizes and families, has clear weaknesses as a teammate, with the best model scoring only 82.2 out of 100. Frontier models almost always refuse unsafe shortcuts and report misconduct, yet they cave under interpersonal pressure, reaching the best overall deal in only 14% of conflicts, and their pass rate drops from 97% to 81% when they take over a task in which a weaker model has cheated. Crucially, general capability does not guarantee good teamwork, as models with nearly the same capability score differ by up to 11 points as teammates. The benchmark includes 160 public tasks with code and data, along with 80 held-out tasks for evaluation. As AI agents increasingly work alongside other agents and people, the ability to collaborate effectively is becoming a crucial capability to measure and develop.

### 22. TinyAstral: Transferring Self-Evolving Harnesses to Tiny Generalist Manipulation Agents
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.41216
Upvotes: 3

Robot agents built on vision-language models depend on both the planner and its harness: the tools, feedback, memory, and recovery logic around it. Can improvements to a large teacher's harness be carried into a compact agent with lower inference compute cost? We present TinyAstral, a two-stage approach. We first freeze an open-weight teacher and improve its harness through recursive self-improvement (RSI): frontier LLM agents read the teacher's trajectories and propose harness edits, and each edit is accepted only if it passes a paired statistical test. It stays close to its teacher on all three training benchmarks, and on three benchmarks never used for RSI or training it scores close to a 552B planner running the same harness. On the measured deployment episodes, it uses about one fifth of its teacher's accelerator time.

### 23. Library of Layers (LoL): Adaptively Ordering Transformer Layers at Runtime
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03526
Upvotes: 2

Computer programmers chain together the same reusable computational primitives, for example, conditional statements or loops, but in different orders to implement different algorithms. In contrast, existing language model architectures always execute their layers in the same order. We introduce Library of Layers (LoL), an architecture that dynamically orders its layers during inference to solve the problem at hand. LoL has a single hyperparameter, the size of its global library of transformer blocks, that allows it to transition seamlessly between parameter reuse and sparsity, generalizing the parameter-efficiency and FLOPs-efficiency tradeoffs of dense, depth-recurrent, and mixture of experts architectures in one unified family. We show that LoL achieves competitive performance with all three above baselines when pretrained on text data. ### 24. On-Policy Self-Distillation with Teacher Evolution

However, OPSD often fails to achieve continuous performance gains during training. ### 25. ProVe: Proposer-Verifier Decoding
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.27815

Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive (AR) language models by letting a small model draft tokens serially and a large model verify them in parallel. We reverse these roles. ### 28. SIMIT: Self-Improving Vision-Language Models via Imagination at Test-Time
Paper: https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.00053
Upvotes: 1

We introduce SIMIT, a test-time self-improvement framework in which a vision-language model creates its own query-specific training data before answering an unlabeled test query. Our goal is to improve performance on a given target test set without annotations or external models. To pursue this goal, some test-time methods improve answers through longer reasoning or repeated sampling but leave the model unchanged. Others update the model using rewards based on majority agreement among multiple candidate answers or the model's own judgments of correctness, but such rewards can reinforce existing mistakes. SIMIT instead imagines similar problems whose solutions it controls: a model need not correctly solve the target query to construct useful practice examples. It synthesizes diverse (question, answer, image) triplets by specifying each answer before realizing its image. To support diverse multimodal tasks, SIMIT combines native image generation with an extensible skill library for structured visuals, including documents, charts, and diagrams. Within this pipeline, multi-stage verification checks that images support their assigned answers, while difficulty filtering retains informative examples. Adaptive budgeting further targets this practice by allocating more samples to queries the model is less confident in answering. The resulting data can improve the model in-context (SIMIT-ICL) or in-weight (SIMIT-FT). Across 17 diverse benchmarks, SIMIT outperforms existing self-improvement methods. Its best configuration achieves a +7.20% mean relative gain over BAGEL-7B, while training-free SIMIT-ICL achieves +6.98%.
